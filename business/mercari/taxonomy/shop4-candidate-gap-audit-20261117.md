## Why the gaps happened

1. **Structural coverage was mistaken for semantic completeness.** 111/111 only requires a nonempty set for each source code. It does not prove coverage of each actual title-described product type. Giga 10651 Outdoor Sports had only a table-tennis candidate; 10952 Deck Boxes/Patio Storage only had garden-furniture Other; 10657 bed frames only included a single-size frame leaf. The versioned CSV's evidence explicitly described category-label semantic proposals requiring later item-level verification, not exhaustive observed catalog coverage.
2. **Taxonomies split products differently.** Tricycles, camera dry cabinets, aquarium cabinets, care chairs and office desk wagons need functional or destination branches absent from supplier-label translations. Singleton and broad Other rows were especially misleading.
3. **Observed source-code/title mismatches exist.** Example: precision screwdriver under Beds/Frames/Bases. These observations are real in the frozen join but do not establish whether the supplier category, join/identity lineage, or stale observation caused them. Do not automatically blame Giga or normalize the wrong source label into an unrestricted business mapping.
4. **Some gaps are classifier false positives or over-specific selections.** A gap means the proposed ID is outside its candidate set; it does not prove that the proposed ID is semantically correct. Four generic folding-bed titles were previously proposed as slat beds despite an existing folding-bed candidate. Inflatable slide titles were sometimes proposed as Other. Recheck these before adding relations.
5. **No full-catalog semantic coverage gate existed at initial dictionary construction.** The current title-only pass reveals the actual missing product kinds. Future releases need source-code coverage *and* title-evidenced subtype coverage, with explicit exceptions.

## What complete must mean

- Structural: 111/111 known source codes covered, no duplicate relations, every exact ID/path valid against the pinned master.
- Current-catalog semantic: nearly all actual products in the user-designated full CSV must receive a stable, appropriate title-supported category. Reconcile all 2,480 Listing IDs; report successful classifications separately from source problems, mapping gaps and genuine title ambiguity. Merely giving every gap a disposition is accounting coverage, not semantic completion. See the accepted full-product baseline amendment below.
- Granularity: review relevant master sibling leaves where the source legitimately spans them (e.g. bed-frame sizes, storage materials, pet species); add only semantically supported branches with applicability guards, not all 7,625 IDs to every code.
- Guarded exceptions: a source-mismatched precision screwdriver must not make Beds → screwdriver a default. Missing/uncertain source identity remains explicit, while unrelated valid rows continue.
- Refresh: rerun candidate-membership validation on the full 2,480 rows and targeted title QA after candidate changes, including previous NO_CHANGE/READY rows; snapshot the new mapping SHA. Do not bulk promote every old MAPPING_GAP just because membership now passes.
- Ongoing limits: this snapshot cannot guarantee unseen future supplier product types. A new title-described product kind creates a visible gap and a reviewed dictionary update. Never silently choose an unrelated Other.
- Distinct queues: existing 103 title ambiguities + 41 source problems are separate from 513 mapping gaps. Expanding candidates alone does not resolve those 144 human exceptions.
- Approval: no Seller changes, DB changes, CSV upload or automatic listing approvals. Keep `NOT_APPROVED`; retain the existing candidate-status enum.
